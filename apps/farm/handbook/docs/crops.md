# Crops: notes by group

Short notes on the crops in the app's library, by group. The library's
numbers (temperatures, water, spacing, days) are what the rules score;
these notes add what the numbers do not say. Sources: ICAR and TNAU
packages of practice, ICRISAT, IRRI Rice Knowledge Bank, FAO ECOCROP.

## Cereals

### Rice

Needs standing water or saturated soil for most of the season. Raise a
nursery for 25 to 30 days and transplant, or sow direct (DSR) to save
water and labour. Alternate wetting and drying saves a quarter of the
water without losing yield.

### Wheat

A rabi crop of cool weather: sow in November in the north and centre.
Late sowing meets heat at grain filling and loses yield every week.

### Maize

Grows in kharif and rabi; does not stand waterlogging, so drain the
field. Good for intercropping with pulses.

## Millets

### Sorghum (jowar)

Kharif and rabi; rabi sorghum on black soils' residual moisture is a
staple of Maharashtra and Karnataka. Stands drought.

### Pearl millet (bajra)

The hardiest cereal: sandy soils, low rain, heat. Short season (75 to
95 days).

### Finger millet (ragi) and foxtail millet

Red soils of Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu; finger millet
stores for years without pests.

## Pulses

### Pigeon pea (red gram, tur)

A long kharif crop (150 to 200 days) with deep roots; the classic
intercrop of the Deccan, with sorghum, groundnut, soybean or cotton.

### Chickpea (Bengal gram)

Rabi, on residual moisture or a light irrigation. Too much water makes it
grow leaves and not pods.

### Green gram and black gram

Short (60 to 90 days); fit after rice on residual moisture, as a summer
crop with irrigation, or between rows of long crops.

## Oilseeds

### Groundnut

Light, well drained soils; needs calcium (gypsum) at pegging.

### Soybean

Black soils of Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra; sow after the rains set in;
drain the field.

### Mustard, sunflower and sesame

Mustard is rabi and stands some salt; sunflower grows in any season
where it is not too wet; sesame is a short kharif crop that hates
waterlogging.

## Fibre and cash crops

### Cotton

A long kharif crop on black soils. Pink bollworm is the main pest: sow in
time, do not keep the crop past its season, use pheromone traps.

### Sugarcane

Ten to eighteen months, a lot of water (1,500 to 2,500 mm); drip saves
much of it.

### Turmeric and chilli

Turmeric is planted in May or June and harvested in about nine months;
chilli is transplanted from a nursery.

## Vegetables

Tomato, onion, brinjal, okra, potato and sweet potato: high value, need
water and care. Tomato and onion prefer the cool season; okra and brinjal
stand heat.

## Fruits and plantation

Mango, guava, papaya, banana, coconut and moringa. Trees take years to
bear: intercrop the young orchard with pulses or vegetables for income.

## New crops from similar climates

Crops new to India are suggested where the plot's climate matches where
they grow well. Little local experience means: try a small area first,
find a market before planting much, and get planting material from a
reliable nursery.

### Dragon fruit

A cactus from Mexico and Central America; stands drought and heat, hates
waterlogging; grown on posts. Now spreading in Gujarat, Maharashtra,
Karnataka and Telangana.

### Avocado

From Mexico; Florida and Mexico's lowland types suit warm, humid places;
needs well drained soil. Grown on a small scale in Karnataka, Kerala and
Tamil Nadu hills.

### Quinoa and chia

Quinoa (Andes) and chia (Mexico) are cool-season crops: in India they fit
the rabi season. Both stand drought; quinoa stands salt.

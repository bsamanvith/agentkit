# Seasons and sowing in India

Starter notes for the farm assistant. Dates are typical for peninsular and
central India; they shift by a few weeks with latitude, altitude and the
year's monsoon. Sources: IMD monsoon onset normals; ICAR and state
agricultural university packages of practice.

## The three seasons

### Kharif

Sown with the south-west monsoon, from June to July, harvested from
September to November. Rice, maize, sorghum, pearl millet, finger millet,
pigeon pea, green gram, black gram, groundnut, soybean, sesame and cotton
are the main kharif crops.

### Rabi

Sown after the monsoon, from October to December, harvested from February
to April. Wheat, chickpea, mustard, rabi sorghum, potato, onion and rabi
pulses. In the Deccan much rabi is grown on the moisture the monsoon left
in black soils, without irrigation.

### Zaid

The short hot season between rabi and kharif, March to June, mostly
irrigated: watermelon, cucumber, green gram, vegetables, fodder.

## When to sow a rainfed crop

### Wait for the rains to set in

The usual rule for kharif is to sow after 50 to 75 mm of rain has fallen
from the onset of the monsoon, so the seed bed is moist down to 15 cm or
more. Sowing on the first shower risks a dry spell that kills the
seedlings.

### Monsoon onset

The monsoon reaches Kerala around 1 June, Telangana and the south of
Maharashtra around 10 June, Mumbai around 11 June, and Delhi around
27 to 30 June. A late onset shortens the season: switch to short-duration
crops (green gram, black gram, foxtail millet, sesame) when sowing slips
past mid-July.

### Residual moisture for rabi

Deep black soils hold 150 to 200 mm of water after a good monsoon. Chickpea,
safflower and rabi sorghum sown in October can finish on it. The later the
sowing, the drier the seed bed: sow as soon as the field can be worked.

## When to sow with irrigation

Irrigation frees the sowing date from the rain, not from the temperature.
Check the crop's temperature range over its whole season: wheat sown after
mid-December in central India meets heat at grain filling in March; a
tomato crop flowering in April and May sets little fruit above about
35 °C.

## Reading a fuzzy date

Farmers give dates as festivals or months: "after Dasara" (October),
"Sankranti" (14 January), "Ugadi" (March or April), "when the rains come".
Treat them as a window of several weeks, not a day.

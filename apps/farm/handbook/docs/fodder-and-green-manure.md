# Fodder, green manure and cover crops

Crops grown to feed animals or the soil rather than to sell. Sources:
ICAR-IGFRI (fodder), ICAR packages of practice, state universities.

## Fodder

### Green fodder crops

- **Fodder sorghum** and **fodder maize**: kharif and summer, cut at
  flowering.
- **Berseem** (Egyptian clover): the rabi fodder of the north and centre;
  several cuts from November to April.
- **Lucerne** (alfalfa): perennial with irrigation, for three to four
  years.
- **Napier hybrid grass** (CO-4, CO-5 and others): perennial; high yields
  with water; planted from stem cuttings on bunds or a plot corner.
- **Cowpea**: a fodder legume to mix with sorghum or maize.

### Fodder trees

Subabul, glyricidia, agathi and moringa leaves supplement green fodder in
the dry months; feed them mixed, not alone.

## Green manure

### Crops

**Dhaincha** (sesbania) and **sunn hemp** are sown before the main crop
and ploughed in at flowering, about 45 days after sowing. They add
nitrogen and organic matter; dhaincha also stands waterlogging and
salinity, so it suits paddy land.

### Green leaf manure

Lopped leaves of glyricidia, pongamia or neem, brought from the border
and ploughed in before transplanting paddy.

## Cover crops

A legume (cowpea, horse gram, green gram) sown on residual moisture after
the main crop keeps the soil covered, adds nitrogen and gives some grain
or fodder.

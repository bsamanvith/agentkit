# Soils of India

How to recognise the main soil types in the field, and how to manage them.
Values are typical, not limits; a soil test (the Soil Health Card) is
better than any description. Sources: ICAR-NBSS&LUP soil descriptions,
state agricultural university guides.

## Recognising a soil

### By colour

Black or dark grey: black soil (regur). Red or reddish brown: red soils.
Brick red with hard pieces that harden in the sun: laterite. Light brown
or grey on plains along rivers: alluvium. Pale sand: desert or coastal
sand.

### By feel

Take a moist pinch and rub it. Gritty is sand; smooth like flour is silt;
sticky, and it rolls into a long thread, is clay. Loam is in between and
crumbles.

### By behaviour

Deep cracks in summer and very sticky when wet: black soil. White crust on
the surface in the dry season: a saline or alkaline (usar) soil. Water
standing for days after rain: poor drainage.

## Black soil (regur)

Clay, rich in calcium, magnesium and potash, low in nitrogen and
phosphorus. pH 7.2 to 8.5. Holds a lot of water but drains slowly.

### Crops

Cotton, soybean, sorghum, pigeon pea, chickpea, sunflower, safflower;
wheat with irrigation.

### Management

Work it at the right moisture: too wet and it puddles, too dry and it is
hard as stone. Broad beds and furrows drain excess monsoon water and keep
the crop off standing water.

## Red soils

Sandy loam to loam, low in nitrogen, phosphorus and organic matter, often
acidic to neutral (pH 5.5 to 7.5). Drain well, hold little water.

### Crops

Groundnut, finger millet, pearl millet, foxtail millet, pulses, castor,
vegetables and fruit trees with irrigation.

### Management

Add farmyard manure or compost every season; mulch to keep moisture.
Lime corrects strong acidity.

## Laterite soils

Acidic (pH 4.5 to 6), leached, low in nutrients; found on hills and the
west coast with heavy rain.

### Crops

Cashew, coconut, arecanut, pineapple, tea and coffee at altitude; rice in
the valleys.

## Alluvial soils

The Indo-Gangetic plain and the river deltas. Loam (silt loam) to clay,
pH 6.5 to 8, fertile.

### Crops

Rice, wheat, sugarcane, maize, pulses, oilseeds and vegetables; most of
India's irrigated cropping.

## Saline and alkaline soils (usar)

A white or grey crust, poor growth in patches, pH above 8.5.

### Crops

Barley, mustard, cotton and sugar beet stand some salt. Rice can be grown
while the soil is being reclaimed.

### Management

Reclaim alkaline soil with gypsum and leaching with good water; improve
drainage; never irrigate with salty borewell water without a test.

## Desert and coastal sands

Sand that holds very little water. Pearl millet, cluster bean and moth
bean in the desert; coconut, cashew and casuarina on coastal sand.
Drip irrigation and mulch make vegetables possible.

## Peaty and marshy soils

Kerala's kari soils, parts of the Sundarbans: waterlogged, very acidic,
rich in organic matter. Rice with drainage and liming.

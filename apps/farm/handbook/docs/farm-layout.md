# Farm layout: gate, paths, sheds and farm ponds

Where the fixed parts of a farm go decides how much walking and carrying
it takes, where the water collects and how much land is left to crop.
Sources: ICAR-IISWC (soil and water conservation), MGNREGA works
guidelines, state agriculture departments' farm pond schemes. The app's
layout rules follow these notes; their numbers are starter values.

## Gate and cart path

### The gate

Put the gate on the side that meets a road or track, so inputs come in and
produce goes out by vehicle. With no road, put it on the side nearest the
village or the neighbour's path that gives the right of way.

### The cart path

A path 3 m wide takes a bullock cart or a small tractor. Run it from the
gate into the plot along the field rows, so it serves every field without
cutting rows short.

## Sheds and stores

A shed for inputs, tools or two or three cattle needs about 6 by 4 m.
Build it just inside the gate, on slightly raised ground, so nothing
crosses the fields to reach it, and keep fertiliser and pesticide in a
locked part away from fodder and water.

## Farm ponds

### What a pond does

A farm pond stores run-off from the rains for a life-saving irrigation in
a dry spell or a second crop, and recharges the groundwater. It suits
rainfed farms most.

### Size

Common sizes under MGNREGA and state schemes are 10 by 10 m, 15 by 15 m,
20 by 20 m and 30 by 30 m, about 3 m deep, with sloping sides. A 15 m
pond 3 m deep holds about 675 m³ (675,000 litres) when full; the sloping
sides make the real volume smaller, about two thirds. Keep a pond under
about a tenth of the plot.

### Where to dig it

At the lowest point of the plot, where run-off collects on its own, or by
the field drain. Not on sandy soil without a lining (it will not hold
water), not near houses without a fence (children), and not under power
lines. Keep 3 m or more between its edge and the boundary for the bund of
dug-out soil.

### Lining and silt

On light soils, line it with a plastic sheet (300 to 500 micron) or a
clay layer. A silt trap (a small pit at the inlet) keeps the pond from
filling with soil; clean the silt out before the rains and spread it on
the fields.

## Bunds and slopes

On sloping land, bunds across the slope (contour bunds) hold the rain on
the field; on flat paddy land, field bunds keep water standing. A border
of trees or a live hedge on the bund holds it together.

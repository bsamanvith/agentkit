# Mixed farming: intercrops, rotation and layout

How to share a plot between crops, and how one season leads to the next.
Row ratios are the ones packages of practice use; local advice may
differ. Sources: ICRISAT and AICRP dryland agriculture recommendations,
state agricultural university packages of practice.

## Why mix crops

Two crops that use the land differently (a tall cereal and a short pulse,
a long crop and a short one, deep roots and shallow) give more from the
same land than either alone, and if one fails the other still pays.

## Intercrops that work

### Sorghum with pigeon pea

Two rows of sorghum to one of pigeon pea. Sorghum is harvested first;
pigeon pea grows on into winter.

### Groundnut with pigeon pea

Four to seven rows of groundnut to one of pigeon pea, on red soils.

### Soybean with pigeon pea

Four rows of soybean to two of pigeon pea, on black soils of central
India.

### Cotton with green gram or black gram

One or two rows of the short pulse between cotton rows; the pulse is
harvested before cotton spreads.

### Maize with soybean or a gram

One row of maize to two of soybean, or green gram between maize rows.

### Young orchards

Pulses, groundnut or vegetables between young mango, guava or moringa
trees for the first three to five years, kept clear of the tree basins.

## Rotation

### Change the family

Growing the same crop, or one of its family, season after season builds
up its pests and diseases and takes the same nutrients out. Follow a
cereal with a pulse, which puts nitrogen back.

### Rice fallows

After kharif rice, black gram, green gram or chickpea on the residual
moisture use land that would lie idle.

## Laying out a plot

### Strips

Cut the plot into strips along its long side, one crop to a strip: easy
to plough, sow, spray and harvest.

### Rows north to south

Run rows north to south so both sides get sun. Put tall crops on the
north side of short ones (in India the midday sun is to the south), so
they do not shade them.

### Spacing

Keep to the spacing of the package of practice. Closer rows do not give
more yield: plants compete for light and water and pests spread.

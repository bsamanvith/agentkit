# Trees for borders, windbreaks and agroforestry

Trees on a farm's boundary give shade, fruit, fodder, timber and a living
fence, and break the wind. They also compete with the crops next to them
for light and water, so choose them by what the farmer needs and plant
them where their shade does the least harm. Sources: ICAR-CAFRI
(Central Agroforestry Research Institute), state forest departments'
farm forestry guides, ICRAF. Starter notes: check species with the local
KVK or forest nursery.

## Planning a border

### Where to plant

In India the sun is in the south for most of the year, so trees on the
north side of a plot shade it least, and trees on the south side shade it
most. A windbreak goes across the prevailing wind: the south-west monsoon
in kharif, and dry westerly winds in summer in the north and centre.
Leave trees out under overhead power lines (low shrubs only) and keep
them a few metres from a neighbour's field.

### Spacing

Border trees go in one row, 3 to 5 m apart for timber and fruit trees,
1 to 2 m apart for a live hedge (glyricidia, subabul). A 3 m band inside
the boundary leaves room for the canopy and a path round the field.

### Root competition

Fast-growing trees take water from the first metres of the field. A
trench 0.5 m wide and 1 m deep between the trees and the crop, dug once
in two or three years, cuts the roots and limits the loss.

## Species by purpose

### Timber and pulpwood

- **Teak** (Tectona grandis): valuable timber, slow (20 years or more);
  well-drained soils, not waterlogged land.
- **Melia dubia** (malabar neem): fast; pulpwood or plywood in 5 to 8
  years; popular in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh.
- **Casuarina**: fast on sandy coastal soils; poles and pulpwood in 3 to
  5 years; a good windbreak on the coast.
- **Eucalyptus**: fast pulpwood, but thirsty and its litter suppresses
  crops: keep it off small plots and away from wells.
- **Poplar**: the farm-forestry tree of Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar
  Pradesh; leafless in winter, so wheat grows under it.

### Fruit

- **Mango**, **tamarind** and **jamun**: long-lived, large; for wide
  borders or the corners.
- **Guava**, **custard apple** and **amla**: small trees that bear in 3 to
  4 years; custard apple and amla stand drought.
- **Coconut**: the border tree of the coastal deltas (Godavari, Krishna,
  Kerala, Konkan), 7.5 to 9 m apart.
- **Drumstick** (moringa): pods and leaves in the first year; light
  shade.

### Fodder, green leaf and fences

- **Glyricidia** and **subabul** (leucaena): live fences and green leaf
  manure; lopped two or three times a year.
- **Neem**: hardy, shade and neem seed for pest control.
- **Pongamia** (karanj): stands drought and saline soils; seed oil for
  biofuel.
- **Bamboo**: a tall hedge and poles; spreads, so keep it in its strip.

## Agroforestry systems

### Trees with field crops

Wide-spaced rows of trees (agri-silviculture) with crops between them for
the first years: poplar with wheat, melia dubia with groundnut or
pulses. As the canopy closes, change to shade-tolerant crops (turmeric,
ginger) or fodder.

### Trees with pasture

Fodder trees with grass (silvi-pasture) on land too poor or dry for
crops: subabul, anjan, khejri in the arid west.

# Water for crops

How much water crops need, what the plot's sources can give, and how to
make it go further. Sources: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56,
Central Ground Water Board, ICAR-IIWM.

## How much a crop needs

### Over the season

Season needs range from about 250 mm (pearl millet, chickpea on stored
moisture) to 1,000 to 2,000 mm (rice, sugarcane, banana). Rain counts
only in part: some runs off, some sinks past the roots. The app counts
four fifths of the rain, and the water a soil held at sowing.

### Critical stages

A crop suffers most from a shortage at flowering and grain or pod
filling. With little water, irrigate at those stages first.

## Sources

### Canals, rivers and tanks

Supply follows the reservoir and the canal's schedule; a tail-end plot
gets less and later. Count an unreliable source at half.

### Borewells

A borewell gives what its yield allows: a 1.5 to 2 inch borewell running a
few hours a day waters an acre or two of irrigated dry crops. Yields fall
in summer.

### Farm ponds

Harvest runoff in the monsoon for one or two protective irrigations in a
dry spell or for a rabi crop.

## Making water go further

### Drip and sprinkler

Drip saves 30 to 50 % of water on vegetables, cotton, sugarcane and
orchards and is subsidised under PMKSY (Per Drop More Crop).

### In the field

Mulch, broad beds and furrows, ridges and furrows, and conservation
furrows every few rows keep rain where it falls.
